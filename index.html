Mission precursors · the lineage

It started on the dance floor.

Eclipse gatherings owe their beginnings to the dance floors of Goa. Then came many transformative gatherings on the ground. A few were pivotal: Solipse in Hungary in '99 and Zambia in '01, Outback Eclipse in South Australia in '02, Oregon Eclipse in '17, and Texas Eclipse in '24, which brought 40,000 people under the shadow.

The pivotal few, among many.

The idea · Both ends of the shadow

One alignment, seen from both ends.

When the Moon slips into Earth's shadow, we see a red Moon. Stand on the Moon at that moment and you see the other side of it: Earth black against the Sun, ringed by every sunrise and sunset on the planet at once. Nobody has watched that from the other end yet.

    Questions

    Before you ask.

    Is this real?

    The eclipses are. Every date here comes from NASA's eclipse tables. The gatherings on Earth are IMXP's events, starting with Iceland Eclipse in 2026. The flights and the Moon depend on commercial spaceflight being built now; we plan around it and open each phase as it becomes possible. If the technology needs more time, a mission moves to the next eclipse: the first view from '33 to '36, the first gathering on the Moon from '36 to '40.

    Can I actually go to the Moon with you?

    That's the goal. Mission '33 flies a community crew in lunar orbit for the first view. Mission '36 is the first gathering on the Moon, a small temporary camp on the surface. By Mission '40 there's permanent infrastructure there, so more of us can go. Everyone starts as a Seeker; Crew is chosen from the community.

    What is the Eclipse Trilogy?

    Iceland '26, Egypt '27 and Australasia '28: three total eclipses in three years, told as three chapters of one gathering, Initiation, Activation and Ascension. They are the Analog phase, the first three missions, and each keeps its own name and site.

    What will it cost?

    Prices for flights beyond Earth orbit aren't public yet. The gatherings on Earth are priced like IMXP's other events. Seekers hear about every price and opening first.

    Why watch from the Moon?

    Because from there, a total lunar eclipse becomes Earth eclipsing the Sun. Earth turns black and its atmosphere lights up as a ring: every sunrise and sunset at once. It's the clearest view there is of how thin and shared our air is.

    Is that ring of light real?

    Yes. Surveyor 3 photographed it from the Moon in 1967, and the Blue Ghost lander caught Earth covering the Sun from the lunar surface in March 2025, with red light falling on the ground around it. Our plates are drawn on that physics.

    What do you leave behind?

    From 2028 on, each eclipse location is a chance to build semi-permanent or permanent infrastructure with the local community: something that stays useful after we leave, and a place people can come back to for years in remembrance of the totality they shared there. On the Moon, Mission '40 is the same idea.

    Who is behind it?

    IMXP, the team behind Iceland Eclipse and the gatherings that follow. Our founder created Texas Eclipse in 2024, which brought 40,000 people under the shadow.
